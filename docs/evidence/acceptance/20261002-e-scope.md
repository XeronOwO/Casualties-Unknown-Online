## Findings and observations

- The breaker's drop capture and the peers' materialization are the two log lines that carry this family:
  `[ItemBuildingDeathDrop] local <type> (id …) at (…) captured for pending block-break report` +
  `Committed(0+N) events=[Break, Drop, BuildingDrop]` on the destroying client, then
  `[ItemSpawn] materializing <type> (id …) at (…), vel (…)` on each peer ~90 ms later — the peer's item
  starts from the breaker's velocity, not from zero.
- The fresh presentation is generated by the game's own `FreshItemDrop` component, and this run proves the
  peers' copies carry it inside its window in both directions (`fresh:3` / `fresh:1` on host, guest and
  alt, ids and positions matching across clients). Source (`reversing/…/FreshItemDrop.cs`): `Start`
  parents a copy of the item's own sprite at scale 1.125 with `Special/ItemOutline` one sorting order
  behind the item; `Update` sets the outline alpha to `clamp01(timeLeft*0.4)` — full alpha for the first
  7.5 s — and `FixedUpdate` raises `gravityScale` from 0 only once `timeLeft < 2`.
- A body placed onto a jump pad consumes it: the pad nearest the second site disappeared while the
  bodies were being moved (the pad count fell 122 → 121 and the next `pad-find` named another pad). Site
  selection therefore places bodies on scanned standing spots, never on the pad, and re-reads the
  distance right before the break.

## Limits

- The visual half of rows 1-4 stays unjudged. The recorded screen→bitmap mapping
  (`x_bmp = 7 + sx*0.8`, `y_bmp = 30 + (720 - sy)*0.8`, client area 1024x576 at window offset (7,30),
  render 1280x720) puts the census-named drop tens of pixels away from the frame's content at this site:
  the marked frame `e-c-guest-marked-zoom.png` shows neither the local body nor any drop under its mark,
  while the same scene's clone sprites are recognisable elsewhere. A rehearsal crop of batch-y's own
  frames at its recorded point is pure black in both its fresh and its control frame
  (`rehearsal-y-drop6-tight.png` / `-after-tight.png`, byte-identical). The camera lead is per-client and
  drifts between the census and the capture, so a future run must calibrate on an in-frame landmark
  (a player sprite or its name tag) before it crops, or judge the row from a surface that renders the
  drop large enough to read.
- The destruction sites are unlit cave rooms; the drop sprites are a few pixels and render dark there,
  which is a second, independent limit on what any crop of these frames can show.
- One session is not a race proof; each row's machine half is read from the state the ends converged to.
- The sites are staged: `site-find.cs` chooses them and `body-place` moves all three bodies, a declared
  substitution for a run whose spawn area is 100+ units from the nearest pad.
