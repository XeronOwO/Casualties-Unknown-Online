# A continue refuses a healthy row for a building the layer no longer has

## Design

Read `docs/design/cross-cutting/saves-and-recovery.md` (what a continue restores, and when a restored fact may be
satisfied rather than refused) and `docs/architecture/save-archive-format.md` (what each cut row means) before
changing the refusal.

The save/continue row stages a trap blast and then cuts. In one fresh batch the restore refused one
`building-health` row and called the world incomplete; the row's own audit read every other fact applied and
zero refused.

## Why the two halves disagree

The cut is taken about a second after the blast. The kernel's `building-health` row is the health the blast
reported; the game then fells buildings whose support the explosion removed, on its own schedule. The cut-time
reconcile (`CutBuildingHealthReconcile`, 2026-11-17) already re-reads the live world so a building missing at
the cut is recorded destroyed — but the fell can land **after** the cut, which leaves a positive-health row for
an entity the next regeneration does not have. The restore then correctly refuses it, and the world is marked
incomplete.

The other candidate is placement drift beyond the lookup's 3 m (`BuildingEntityLookup`); the 2026-11-17
readings measured drift up to about 2 m, and this row was not measured.

## Options

- A — cut-time settle: the trap-blast row waits for the game's support-loss pass before cutting (a bounded
  wait on "no structural collapse pending", or cutting at the next layer-stable frame). Consequence: the row
  costs a frame or two and the race is gone, but the cut gets a new timing rule to document.
- B — restore-side rule: a positive-health row whose position has no building in the regenerated layer is
  recorded as destroyed when the kernel's run also reports the entity gone. Consequence: a genuinely lost
  damaged building would stop being a red, which is exactly the signal we want; needs the kernel's entity
  lifetime, not just the position.
- C — measure first: run the focused row N times on fresh worlds, record the refusal rate and the distance
  between the recorded cell and the nearest live building in the cases that refuse. Consequence: no fix yet,
  but the next step is chosen by a number.

Recommendation: C, then A if the refusals cluster on rows cut within a second of a collapse; B only if a
refused row turns out to have its building standing a few metres away.

## Measurement design (2026-11-18)

The three lines the fix will be built from, written before the first edit:

1. **The player's action and expectation** — the host stands in a continued world, places a mine next to the
   base, lets it blow, saves, leaves to the menu and continues. They expect the damaged world back: the holes
   they made, the buildings that fell, the geysers still venting where they were.
2. **What the game itself runs** — the explosion calls
   `WorldGeneration.CreateExplosion` (`reversing/release-1.0.2/Assembly-CSharp/WorldGeneration.cs:5449`,
   `buildingEntity.health -= param.structuralDamage * Random.Range(0f, 2f);`); a building whose support is
   removed is then felled by the game's own support-loss pass, which reports nothing, on its own schedule.
3. **How the remote case reaches the same chain** — the host's cut reads the kernel's rows
   (`WorldCutWriter`), the continue replays them (`RestoredWorldFactReplay` →
   `WorldBuildingEntitySync.ApplyRemoteBuildingEntityHealth` at
   `src/CasualtiesUnknownOnline.GameAdapter/World/WorldBuildingEntitySync.cs:242`), and the entity is found by
   `BuildingEntityLookup.At` (`src/CasualtiesUnknownOnline.GameAdapter/World/BuildingEntityLookup.cs:18`).
   When that lookup misses and the row's health is positive, the row is refused and the restore reports the
   world incomplete.

The measurement adds one diagnostic to the refusal branch: it names the nearest live building (include-inactive)
and its distance, so a refusal says whether the layer has no building or one a few metres off. The decision rule:

- refusals with no building within 25 m → the fell landed after the cut → A (settle the cut) or B (restored
  destroyed fact), chosen by whether the cut's blast is within one second of the save;
- refusals with a building 3-10 m away → placement drift → widen the lookup with that reading;
- no refusal in five fresh runs → the rate is under one in five; keep the ticket open with the measured rate and
  leave the rule alone.
