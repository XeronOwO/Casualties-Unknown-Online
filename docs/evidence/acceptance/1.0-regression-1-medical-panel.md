### The bandage treatment: fixture built, completion blocked (2026-11-18)

A by-name case for the bandage row was written and driven four times against the deployed build
(`.acceptance/medical-bandage3` … `medical-bandage6`). What already reads correctly on the patient's own
client:

- a real wound through the game's own damage entry: `wound-seed limb=1 force=300` answered
  `skinHealth -12.2, bleedAmount 35.855` (the force has to clear `Limb.ImpactDamage`'s thresholds,
  `reversing/release-1.0.2/Assembly-CSharp/Limb.cs:442-447`);
- the bandage taken and started through the wound view: `item-provide type=bandage mode=create` answered
  `slot 0, onBody true`, and `limb-use type=bandage mode=use` answered `usableOnLimb true, actuallyUsable
  true` on the wounded limb;
- the limb readback: `limb-read source=local limb=1` answers the bleed before and after.

What cannot be read yet is the treatment's effect: the item's minigame never completes. The committed
in-client declaration `bandage-rub6` is not registered by the client (the driver answers "the eval
declaration 'bandage-rub6' was sent but the type is still not in the client's evaluator"), and the
per-frame recipe workaround (`bandage-step`, one hand movement per driver round trip, 160 calls) left the
game's own counter at `{"ran":true,"actions":0}` — the hand moved but the game performed no bandage
action, so the bleed only changed by the wound's own healing (35.9 → 33.9 over the row).

The row therefore stays **not driven**, with the blocker as its own High ticket
([a minigame's click-start edge never arms](../../backlog/done/1.0-minigame-click-edge-never-arms.md)).
The declaration channel behind it was repaired the same night
([acceptance declarations](../../backlog/done/1.0-acceptance-declarations-not-registered.md)): `declare bandage-rub6`
now answers `{"ok":true,"declared":true}` and `bandage-rub-state` drove 1,200 frames, while the game's own
counter still read `actions 0` because its click-start edge never arms (`keyDownAnswers 0` over 25,045 key
queries, `.acceptance/decl-bandage-live`). The case itself is written and parked in the registry with that
reason; it must be re-registered when the edge is fixed and the row must then read a stopped bleed, not a
smaller one.

The tourniquet and splint rows are still unread and share the same dependency where their gesture spans
frames.

### The bandage and tourniquet treatments (2026-11-18)

- The bandage row passes once the fixture taps the attack key instead of holding it: `medical-bandage`
  (`.acceptance/bandage-taps1`, case 40.9 s) drove the item's own minigame until the wound's bleed reached
  zero on the patient's own client. The row is registered by name; the tap rule (a held key has one edge)
  is on the minigame ticket.
- The tourniquet row is new: `medical-tourniquet` (`.acceptance/tourniquet5`, case 1.2 s, total 2.1 s,
  PASS on the deployed `0.1.0+fb1d1d733`). The fixture picks a limb whose game chain reaches beyond
  itself, seeds a real wound (`wound-seed limb=13 force=200` → the wound shows), frees a slot through the
  game's own drop, takes a tourniquet, and applies it through the wound view's own use entry
  (`limb-use type=tourniquet mode=use`, `actuallyUsable true`).
- Reading, on the member's own client: the treated lower leg reads `skin 14.655, bleedAmount 16.264,
  blockedBleeding true` with the tourniquet on it; the game's own `GetLowerLimbs` chain for that limb is
  `[13, 14]`; the foot reads `blockedBleeding true` and carries no tourniquet of its own; every limb above
  the tourniquet reads `blockedBleeding false`. The limb's `bleedAmount` stays because it is the wound —
  the flag is what the body's summed bleed speed multiplies by zero. The row asserts the exact set, so a
  tourniquet that blocked the wrong limbs would fail it.
- Still unread in this batch: the injection, the splint treatment, the CPR controls and the shrapnel
  session.

### The splint treatment (2026-11-18)

- The splint row is new: `medical-splint` (`.acceptance/splint2`, case 1.3 s, total 2.3 s, PASS on the
  deployed `0.1.0+55f00d669`). The fixture breaks a limb through the game's own `Limb.BreakBone` (the one
  declared setup substitution), frees a slot through the game's own drop, gives the body a splint, selects
  the limb and applies it through the wound view's own use entry (`limb-use type=splint mode=use`,
  `actuallyUsable true`).
- Reading: the treated limb carries the game's own `SplintLimb` component and reads `splinted true`; the
  carried splint is gone (the native action destroys the item). The removal goes through the wound view's
  special action (`limb-use type=splint mode=special`, the native `WoundSpecialAction` → `TakeOff`), after
  which the limb reads `splint false` and `splinted false`, and the item is back on the body.
- The first run of this row failed before the reading: the recipe's special mode searched the body for a
  carried splint first, which the application had already destroyed. The native removal needs no item, so
  the recipe now dispatches `mode=special` before the held-item search (`55f00d669`); no assertion was
  relaxed.
