# A trigger event waits for the whole living team

- Status: Review — the rule is the user's proposal (2026-11-14); the reference mod's behaviour is the
  counter-example, our own trigger surfaces have not been read yet.
- Priority: Medium

## Design

Read: `docs/design/game/worldgen-and-layers.md` (the layer transitions and their triggers) and
`docs/design/mechanics/world-interaction.md` (who owns an authoritative event).

## What the user proposed (2026-11-14, translated)

"In Together, when a trigger event fires, ANY one player triggering it teleports everyone there and fires
it for all. I think ours should instead require every living player to be in position before it fires —
that feels more like multiplayer. Or you may have a better design; we can discuss it later."

## Why the difference matters

A trigger that one player can fire on the team's behalf turns the team into one player's passengers: the
others are teleported mid-action, or lose the layer they were exploring. Requiring everyone present keeps the
group's own decisions in the loop — but it must not deadlock: a dead or disconnected player, or one who
cannot reach the trigger at all, must not block the team forever.

## Options (for the discussion the user asked for)

- **A — all living present.** The trigger arms only when every living, connected member stands inside the
  trigger's own area; the first member to press it fires it for all. Dead and disconnected members are not
  counted. Recommended: it matches the proposal and has one rule to explain.
- **B — all present, with a timeout.** Same as A, but the armed state names who is missing on the host's
  screen, and after a long window (the run's own layer timer) the trigger becomes single-player-fires.
  Protects against a stuck member at the cost of a second rule.
- **C — majority vote.** The trigger fires when most living members are present and one presses it. Not
  recommended: a majority can override a minority's exploration, which is the complaint about the
  reference mod.

## What to read before the first probe

1. Our own trigger surfaces: which events can teleport or advance a layer today, and where their gates live
   (the run's layer descent, the layer-end panel, the pod).
2. What the game itself considers "in position" for each trigger (a collider, a distance, a scene state).
3. How a member's presence and aliveness reach the host (the roster and the vitals the host already holds).

## Acceptance rows

1. Two clients in a layer, one at the trigger and one away: the trigger does NOT fire when the near one
   presses it, and the host's own state names the missing member.
2. Both at the trigger: the first press fires it for both, and both read the same layer state afterwards.
3. A dead member does not block: with one member dead and the other present, the living member's press fires
   the trigger (read from both clients' own layer state).
4. A disconnected member does not block (the roster no longer carries it).

## What must not change

- No client is teleported without its own owner's input as the trigger; the rule gates WHEN a press fires,
  not WHERE a player may stand.
- The descent's own mechanism (the host's baseline and the members' generation) is untouched.
