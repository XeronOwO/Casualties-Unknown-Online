## What the player sees

Two people are in the same run and see different worlds. One walks through a wall the other cannot pass; a geyser that
is a water vent on one screen is a dry rock on the other; a save written on one side restores onto terrain the other side
does not have. Nothing is logged on either side, because each client believes its own table.

## What the diagnosis now answers

`tests/CasualtiesUnknownOnline.AcceptanceSuite/Cases/WorldFingerprintCase.cs`'s `DiagnoseAsync` used to stop at the first
differing row. It now counts the differing rows in every band and, for the first and the last differing row, the differing
cells plus how many of them the member reads as `00`. Read the next failing batch like this:

- many rows, all differing cells `00` on one side → that side's band is still filling in (a residency/timing question, and
  the row must wait for a settled table or compare a resident band);
- a handful of cells, both sides non-zero → a structural divergence between the two worlds (a product defect, and the
  row is right to fail).

## Next probe

The product writes its own `[WorldFingerprint]` line at every world entry. In a failing batch, compare the two clients'
*entry-time* lines (not the fresh device call): if they agree at entry and differ later, the divergence is post-entry state
(a mutation, or a region the game unloads at different times on each side); if they disagree at entry, generation is on
trial. The `log-tail` driver action returns the oldest matched line first, so the entry line is the `line0` of a
`-Text 2` read.
