# Save after trap damage drops building health

- Status: Done (2026-11-17) — the cut now names the buildings the live world still has, and the
  merged save/continue row applies every row: `2 applied / 0 refused world-entity row(s)`, `0 row(s)
  not taken`, `restore complete`.
- Priority: High
- Created: 2026-11-16

## Design

The restore contract is in `docs/design/cross-cutting/saves-and-recovery.md`: the cut carries the
host's world facts and Continue repairs them per entry; a cut names only the buildings the live world
still has. The archive layout and the restore audit live in
`docs/architecture/save-archive-format.md`.

## Reading

The merged full suite (`.acceptance/mergedfull1/report/report.md`) placed
`save-continue` after `mine-blast`. The save itself passed behaviourally, but
the host logged two errors while restoring the continued world:

```
[SaveFacts] the live world did NOT take 2 of 3 restored world-entity row(s) —
the regenerated layer has no such entity where the cut recorded it, and those
facts are NOT in the live world.
```

The cut at `.acceptance/merged1/saves/w-20261115-bf62/live.cuoz` carries:

- `trap-consumption` at `(10,511)`;
- `building-health` at `(11,512)`, health `1895.8866`;
- `building-health` at `(-63,446)`, health `0`;
- `trap-state` at `(10,511)` kind `31`, phase `2`;
- `trap-state` at `(10,511)` kind `1`, phase `3`.

The live world took the trap consumption and refused both building-health
rows. The blocks, keypads, geysers, recipe unlocks, radiation line, and items
all restored.

## Why it matters

The restore audit is deliberately strict: a missing world-entity row means the
continued world is not the saved one. The full suite used to avoid the red by
running `save-continue` before `mine-blast`; that was a test-order isolation,
not a product fix. A player who saved after a trap explosion lost the damaged
building's health and the next Continue reported an incomplete restore.

## Root cause (2026-11-17)

A building the blast fells through its own support loss keeps the blast-time
health row the kernel reported. The game reports the damage the explosion
deals, then destroys a building whose support it removed, and that destruction
reports nothing. The cut stored the stale positive health; the restore looked
for the entity in the regenerated layer, found none, refused the row and called
the world `INCOMPLETE`.

Two other shapes were found in the same work and are part of the same restore
account:

- a consumed one-shot trap the regenerated layer never recreated (a mine the
  player staged) was refused although the world state was right (nothing armed);
- the fixed 5 s geyser readiness window could expire while the live set was
  still filling, refusing rows that arrived later.

## Fix (2026-11-17)

- The cut reconciles its building-health rows against the live world at cut
  time (`CutBuildingHealthReconcile`): a named building that no longer stands
  is recorded destroyed (health 0), a moved building records its live health,
  and with no live world the kernel's facts are kept unchanged.
- A consumed one-shot trap the restored layer does not have is satisfied, not
  refused (`TrapVisualReplay`); state rows keep refusing on a missing entity.
- The geyser readiness wait holds while the live count is still growing and
  releases when it settles or the ceiling is reached (`GeyserFillWait`).
- Design pages `docs/design/cross-cutting/saves-and-recovery.md` and the code
  audit carry the three rules.

## Acceptance

1. Reproduce with `save-continue` immediately after `mine-blast`; the restore
   must either apply both building-health rows or classify an already-destroyed
   building as intentionally absent without an error.
2. The restored live world must carry the same building health for every
   building the cut names, or the archive must omit rows that cannot exist in
   a regenerated layer.
3. The full suite keeps `save-continue` before the damage rows until the
   restore behaviour is fixed and covered by a dedicated case.
4. No new wire shape is required; this is the restore half of the existing
   world-entity facts, not a new message.
